Legend: insight terms and how to investigate

These labels describe statistical patterns, not confirmed root causes.

Extreme outlier pattern

The maximum is much larger than P95 (in this engine, often at least 3× P95). Example: P95 = 2 units and max = 10 units. The largest difference may be concentrated in a few values; inspect the top assets and verify source values/units.

Concentrated mismatches

A small share of compared values mismatch (the generator classifies ≤10% as isolated). Example: 10 mismatches out of 411 = 2.43%. Check the specific affected assets first.

Widespread mismatches

A large share of values mismatch (the generator classifies ≥50% as widespread). Example: 69 of 69 values = 100%. Check shared assumptions, case setup, conventions, or broad operating-state differences before treating each item separately.

P95 exceeds tolerance

The 95th-percentile absolute delta is above the configured mismatch tolerance. Differences are not limited to just one maximum; inspect mismatch rate and affected assets, then compare the configured tolerance and units.

Heavy upper tail

P95 is much larger than the median, or P99 is much larger than P95. Example: median = 1, P95 = 8, P99 = 30. A subset of values has much larger differences; inspect the distribution and ranked assets.

Mean much larger than median

A few large values may pull the mean upward. Example: median = 2 but mean = 15. Review P95, maximum, and top assets; do not use the mean alone to describe a typical difference.

Directional pattern

Most signed deltas share a sign. Here signed delta means Case B − Case A (except where a metric-specific comparison convention applies). Example: 95% positive means Case B is higher for most mismatches. Check whether this aligns with expected model or operating changes.

Relative outlier pattern

Relative deltas are large compared with typical relative differences. Small baseline values can inflate percentages, so inspect absolute and relative deltas together and confirm denominator/baseline values.

MAD

Median absolute deviation: the median distance from the median delta magnitude. A zero MAD can occur when at least half the values sit at the same magnitude (often zero); it does not mean every value matches.

Affected assets

Distinct assets with at least one mismatch, divided by distinct compared assets when asset IDs are available. This is not always the same as the number of mismatching metric values.

Severe-threshold exceedance

Count of values exceeding the configured severe threshold. Interpret it using the metric's unit and configuration; a threshold is not automatically a universal engineering limit.

Recommended investigation sequence
  1. Confirm the metric, unit, tolerance, severe threshold, and comparison convention.
  2. Check mismatch rate and affected-asset coverage to determine whether the issue is localized or broad.
  3. Compare median, MAD, P95, P99, and maximum to understand typical versus tail behavior.
  4. Inspect the largest-delta assets and compare their Case A, Case B, and signed-delta values.
  5. For relative outliers, inspect baseline values because small denominators can inflate percentages.
  6. Compare related metrics and nearby assets, then record a root cause only after checking the underlying inputs and operating assumptions.
